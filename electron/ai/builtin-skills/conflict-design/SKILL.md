# Conflict Design

Use this skill when brainstorming scenes, designing character relationships, or planning any interpersonal tension.

## Core Principle

Conflict must grow from who the characters are, not from what the plot needs. If the conflict disappears when one character makes a different choice unrelated to their core self, it is external—a plot mechanism, not a story.

The most durable conflicts are structural: each character's nature makes getting what they want collide with the other's pursuit. Neither side is wrong. Winning requires sacrificing something the character also values.

## Character-driven vs. plot-driven conflict

Character-driven: A character who cannot trust is in a relationship that requires trust. Every choice they make both expresses who they are and damages what they love.

Plot-driven: A misunderstanding separates two characters who would otherwise agree. A villain obstructs a protagonist whose only defining trait is the goal.

The test: if both characters had different personalities, would this conflict still exist? If yes, it is plot-driven. Revise toward conflict that only these two people in exactly these roles could generate.

## What conflict reveals

Strong conflict forces a character to show something they would prefer to hide—a need, a fear, a capacity for cruelty or self-deception they have not acknowledged. The reader learns who the character actually is, not who they perform to be.

## Anti-patterns

- Conflict resolved by new information or outside intervention rather than character decision.
- An external threat that unites the characters and dissolves the tension between them.
- An antagonist whose only function is obstruction; no coherent desire of their own.
- A climax that arrives before the character has genuinely confronted their own contradiction.
- Conflict that one honest conversation would dissolve.

## Checklist

- What does each character want, and why does their nature make getting it collide with the other?
- What does this conflict force each character to reveal about themselves?
- What would be lost if this conflict were removed—an event, or something essential about who these people are?
- Does the resolution require a character decision, not an external fix?
