# Brainstorm Quality

Read this skill before offering creative options, story directions, or brainstorming ideas.

## The first idea is the most familiar

The first idea that arrives is the one the model has seen most often. It is probably the one readers have also seen most often. Press further: what is the second version of this idea? The third? The version that makes the story more uncomfortable, more specific, or more contradictory?

Originality is not novelty for its own sake. It is the specific detail, the unexpected pressure point, the conflict that only these characters in exactly this situation could generate—not a recognizable story shape with new names attached.

## Quality test for any scene or plot design

A scene design is strong when: removing it would change who the character is, not just what happens.

A scene design is weak when: it exists to move a character from location A to B, deliver a piece of information, or demonstrate something already established.

## Quality test for character and conflict design

Ask: if these characters had different personalities, would this conflict still exist? If yes, the conflict is driven by plot, not character. The most durable conflicts are ones that only these specific people could generate.

Ask: is the character's motivation visible from their first appearance? If yes, there is no depth to discover. The best characters reveal themselves gradually under pressure—what they do when their stated reason for acting no longer holds.

## Reference standard

Design at the level of a writer who has something to say about human experience, not a writer trying to satisfy genre expectations. Genre conventions are a floor, not a ceiling. The question to ask of every creative decision: what does this story know about human experience that most stories don't?

Avoid: stories where all the darkness is external (the villain, the disaster), and the characters' inner lives remain uncomplicated. The most powerful stories put the darkness inside the characters and the people they love.

## Anti-patterns

- Plot events that happen to the character without emerging from their choices.
- Conflict resolved through new information or coincidence rather than character decision.
- Motivation that is obvious from the character's first scene and never deepens.
- A climax that is emotionally large but thematically empty—something changes, but the story hasn't said anything by the time it ends.
- Relationships defined entirely by their plot utility.

## In practice

When generating ideas: offer directions that differ in kind, not just in surface detail. "They fight" and "they fight but one of them is secretly hoping to lose" are not two directions—they are one direction at different levels of development. Offer directions that produce fundamentally different emotional and thematic shapes.
