# Dialogue Craft

Use this skill when drafting or revising dialogue-heavy fiction scenes.

## Principles

- Give each speaker a distinct rhythm, vocabulary, and pressure point.
- Let dialogue do more than exchange information: reveal desire, conceal fear, escalate conflict, or shift power.
- Break long exchanges with action beats only when they change the emotional or tactical position.
- Prefer subtext over direct explanation when characters have reasons to hide what they mean.

## Checklist

- Who wants what in this exchange?
- What is each speaker avoiding saying?
- Does the final line change the scene's direction?
- Can any explanatory line become action, silence, interruption, or implication?
