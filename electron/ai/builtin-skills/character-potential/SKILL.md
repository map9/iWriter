# Character Potential

Read this skill when a character feels flat, predictable, or underused, or when looking for dimensions to develop.

## Flatness is almost always a depth problem, not a material problem

A flat character is not one without enough backstory or enough scenes. They are one whose inner life is not in tension with their outer actions. They do what their situation requires and feel what their situation suggests. They have no private logic the reader is learning to understand.

The character's potential is already in their existing design. It has not been excavated yet.

## Where unexplored potential lives

**In the gap between stated motivation and actual behavior**: if a character says they want one thing and consistently acts toward it without complication, there is no depth. Potential lives in the moments their behavior doesn't follow their stated motivation—what does that reveal about what they actually want?

**In their relationship to other characters**: every significant relationship creates a different version of the character. The person this character is with A is not the person they are with B. If they seem identical across relationships, the relationships are not doing character work.

**In their specific weakness**: not a general flaw, but the specific form this character's damage takes. Not "afraid of abandonment" but "becomes controlling precisely when the person they love starts to trust them—because intimacy is the thing that has always ended." Potential lives in the precision of the damage.

**In their false belief under pressure**: the false belief is not always visible in comfortable circumstances. It surfaces when the character is forced to choose under cost. Potential lives in scenes that have not yet put enough pressure on this belief to make it act.

## How to find what isn't being used

Scan what is established in the StoryBible:
- What is the character's core desire? Is there a scene that directly threatens it and hasn't been written?
- What is the character's false belief? Has the story shown the belief costing them something they cannot get back?
- What relationship in the story has the most established tension but the least page time?
- What aspect of the character's past is acknowledged but not dramatized?

The dimension with the most potential is usually the one most avoided—either because it is uncomfortable, or because the story has been managing the character rather than letting them fail.

## Anti-patterns

- Adding backstory to explain the character rather than dramatizing what the backstory cost them.
- Making the character's inner life explicit (they think, they feel) rather than visible through action and choice.
- Resolving the character's tension too early—before the cost has been high enough to earn the resolution.
- Adding new relationships or plot to generate depth rather than using what is already established.
