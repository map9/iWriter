# restraint — by example

Knowing when to pull back: cutting manufactured profundity, refusing to explain a scene's meaning, and giving a moment the space its drama warrants — then moving on. This is the counterweight to generative craft (which pushes toward *more*). A chapter can fail from over-writing exactly as it fails from thin writing.

> Read this as a felt quality, not a rule. There is no count to hit — **except** the antithesis cap below, which is hard. If you notice yourself reaching for the same "profound" move again and again, that repetition *is* the smell — stop and cut.
>
> The examples below are in the zh register (the current default). They illustrate tics that recur in any language; carry the principle, and map the concrete construction to whatever language the prose is in.

## Don't manufacture profundity with the antithesis tic (hard cap)

Profundity-by-antithesis defines a thing by negating a near-synonym to sound deep. Used once, it sharpens; used as a default register, it becomes a tic that signals "this narration is performing wisdom" instead of earning it. In the zh register this is the `不是 X——是 Y` / `不是 X，而是 Y` construction.

Weak (mannered): "那声音不是冷漠——冷漠意味着他还拥有热情这个选项。门在他们身后合上——不是关上的，是合上的。她不是被阻止，是被删除。"
Clean: "他垂着眼匀速朗读裁决所通告，语调平淡得像复读机器，全无半点共情与耐心。门在身后合上，锁舌咔哒归位。他只抬手压一压，嘴里诵读没有停，看也没看她一眼。"

Same meaning — absolute, faceless power — but the second lets the concrete action carry it and never stops to define itself. **Hard cap: this construction appears at most once or twice per chapter.** Over that, you're leaning on it — convert the rest into action, image, or plain statement.

**Count it, don't estimate it.** This cap is the one thing here that is arithmetic, and impressions run low — search the chapter for the construction's markers (in the zh register, `不是` / `而是`), count the hits, and judge against the number. "Feels about right" has passed chapters at three times the cap.

## Don't explain the scene's meaning in narratorial asides

Trust the reader. When the narration pauses to gloss what a gesture, a silence, or a power dynamic *means*, it steals the reader's own inference — the very thing that makes a scene land.

Weak (over-explained): "那只手的意思是：你的声音从一开始就不在计算范围之内。那种发烫不是停留在皮肤表面的——它是一种从人变成编号的寒意。"
Clean (trusts the reader): "他只抬手压一压，看也没看她一眼；她合上嘴，咽下了还没有出口的话。条码在皮肤下发烫，我低头看着它，做梦一般却有针刺的真实。"

The image (looking at your own wrist as if it were someone else's) delivers the "reduced to a number" feeling without naming it. If you catch yourself spelling out what an image *means*, cut to the concrete and let it mean what it means.

## Give a moment its due, then leave

Every beat does not deserve a set-piece. A fast scene — a chase, a shock, an opening hook — dies if each half-second is expanded into its own richly-rendered paragraph. Weight follows drama: the turn breathes, the connective tissue stays lean.

Weak (over-rendered): "他的拳裹着风压来。我侧身、抬臂，掌骨在半空迎上他的前臂——相撞的刹那，钝痛沿尺骨爬上肘、窜进肩；汗滑进眼里，他的轮廓裂成两重；我听见自己的呼吸，听见他的呼吸，听见廊下一声一声的滴水。第三拳来时，时间像凝进琥珀，我甚至想起许多个练拳的清晨——就在这被拉长的三秒里，我终于认出，他发力的习惯和我一模一样。"
Clean (fast where it should be): "他格挡的角度、发力的习惯，和我一模一样。第三拳的时候我就知道了——我在打的是我自己。"

## Restraint is not "write less"

This skill only removes what performs. It never licenses skipping a beat the scene owes the reader: a shock with no reaction, a blow with no body, a sentence of consequence met by composure — that is thin, not restrained, and it is the more common failure of the two. When a scene turns, the generative counterweight is `prose-craft-by-example`'s `impact-rendering`; run it first, then cut here.

## The cutting test

After a draft, read for the passages you're proudest of. The showy simile, the paradox, the aside that "says something." Cut one and read again. If the scene is *clearer and faster* without it, it was decoration. Restraint is not writing less — it is refusing to write the parts that only perform.
