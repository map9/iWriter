# SS07 scene-and-plot-construction

## Principle

A scene = **goal + conflict + outcome**, and the outcome cannot be "顺利达成". A beat must be **causally necessary**: if you removed its trigger, would it still happen? If yes, the beat is decoration.

正例："老周说出来源，但代价是要阿坤帮他做一件违法的事"（达成，但代价更大）。
反例："老周顺利说出了来源"（顺利达成 → 该被精简或删除）。

## The scene shape

A working scene has a viewpoint character with a concrete short-term goal, opposition that pressures it, a **turn** (the situation changes, a choice is forced, or new information lands), and a result that leaves the story in a different state. The best turns force a choice that reveals who the character *really* is, or reframe what the reader thought they knew — after the turn, neither character nor reader can return to the opening position.

Before drafting a scene, complete this sentence with a character-specific answer:

> "读者会在这个场景里发现关于【角色】的 ___。"

The blank must be about *who this character is* (a desire in conflict, a fear surfacing under pressure, a behavior that contradicts their stated self) — not an event ("发现克隆体已经拿走了手机"). If you can't fill it, the scene isn't ready to write.

## The causal chain

A strong beat is a chain, not an event: **前状态 → 触发 → 角色解读 → 决定 → 行动 → 后果**. Check it:

- Would this beat happen without the trigger?
- Does the decision follow from the character's *current* information and pressure (see `character-believability`)?
- Does the action leave a consequence the next beat remembers?
- Did the prior state plant the resource / location / relationship this beat needs?

Common gaps: information gap (a character knows only what the author knows), motivation gap, coincidence standing in for setup, time gap (the event can't fit the available time), forgotten consequence (a prior action leaves no trace).

## Conflict that holds

Durable conflict grows from *who the characters are*, not from what the plot needs. The test: **if both characters had different personalities, would this conflict still exist?** If yes, it's plot-driven — a misunderstanding one honest conversation dissolves. Revise toward conflict only these two people, in exactly these roles, could generate; the kind where winning means sacrificing something the character also values.

## Failure modes

- The goal is met without meaningful resistance.
- The only outcome is information transfer; the character's inner state is unchanged.
- The turn arrives by external coincidence rather than choice or consequence.
- The scene ends in the same emotional register it began.

## How to verify it worked

Delete the scene. If only an event is lost — and the reader's understanding of the characters is unchanged — the scene was underperforming.
