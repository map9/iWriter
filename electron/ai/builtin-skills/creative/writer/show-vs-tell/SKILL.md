# Show vs Tell

Use this skill to decide when the reader should experience a feeling and when the narration should move quickly.

## Show

Show through observable pressure:

- Behavior: what the character does instead of what they claim.
- Dialogue: what they avoid saying, repeat, or say too late.
- Body language: breath, stillness, movement, interruption, misdirected attention.
- Sensory focus: what the character notices under stress.

Use show for high-emotional-tension moments, reversals, betrayals, confessions, choices, and scenes where the reader needs to infer the truth.

## Tell

Tell when compression is the point:

- Background exposition.
- Time jumps.
- Low-stakes transitions.
- Information the reader needs but should not linger inside.

## Conversion Pattern

Replace a conclusion with evidence.

- Telling: "He was angry and left."
- Showing: "He pushed the chair back so hard it struck the wall, then walked out without looking at her."

## Balance

Do not show everything. A scene made only of evidence can become slow or coy. Use telling to bridge, show to land.
