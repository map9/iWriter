# Show vs Tell — by example

Deciding when the reader should *experience* a feeling and when the narration should move quickly.

## Show through observable pressure

At high-tension moments — reversals, betrayals, confessions, choices, anything where the reader should *infer* the truth — replace the conclusion with evidence: behavior (what they do vs. what they claim), dialogue (what they avoid, repeat, or say too late), body language (breath, stillness, interruption, misdirected attention), and what the character notices under stress.

反例（tell）："他很生气，然后走了。"
正例（show）："他把椅子往后一推，撞在墙上，没看她一眼就走了出去。"

Same fact — he leaves angry — but the second lets the reader arrive at "angry" themselves, which is why it lands harder.

## Tell when compression is the point

Background exposition, time jumps, low-stakes transitions, information the reader needs but should not linger inside — tell these plainly and move on.

反例（over-shown）：把三年的时间跨度逐幕演出来，节奏塌掉。
正例（told）："接下来的三年，码头换了两任老板，阿坤一次都没再碰过那张照片。"

## Balance

Don't show everything — a scene made only of evidence turns slow or coy. Tell to bridge, show to land.
