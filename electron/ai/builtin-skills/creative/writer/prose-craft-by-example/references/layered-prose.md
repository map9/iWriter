# Layered Prose — by example

Thin vs. dense prose: how a passage can carry situation, character, and undercurrent together — shown by example, **not** as a per-sentence quota.

> Read this as a felt quality, not a rule. There is no count to hit. "每个句子必须同时承担两个功能" is the anti-pattern — it produces cramped, overworked sentences. Aim instead to write *like* the dense examples below.

## Thin vs. dense

Thin prose is not prose with few events — it's prose where a passage earns only one thing at a time: it names a feeling, then separately shows a body; it sets a scene without revealing the character's state; it moves someone from place to place and does nothing else.

反例（thin）："她走进房间的时候很紧张。房间很大，灯很亮。"
正例（dense）："她推开门，日光灯先一步打在脸上，她还没来得及把表情整理好。"

Same facts — she enters, the room is bright — but the dense version also shows her preparing to perform, the failure of it, the light as something that *acts on* her, and vulnerability without naming it.

## The removal test (not a counting test)

Remove a sentence or a passage. What is lost?
- If only a fact is lost — a plot beat, a location, an exposition line — it was doing one thing. Ask whether it could also carry character or undercurrent.
- If a fact, an emotional texture, *and* a character implication go at once, it has earned its place.

This is a diagnostic you run when a passage feels flat, not a bar every sentence must clear.

## Character through the passing detail

Character is revealed most efficiently through what someone notices, what they do when no one is watching, and what they *don't* do when doing it would be easy — a glance, a hesitation, an automatic motion — while the scene advances anyway. Prefer that to a sentence that stops to announce "她是那种……的人".
