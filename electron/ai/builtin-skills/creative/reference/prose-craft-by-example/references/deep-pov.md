# Deep POV — by example

Writing close viewpoint: all perception filtered through the viewpoint character's knowledge, mood, and language.

## What filtering means in practice

Filtering is not just removing knowledge the character can't have — it's ensuring what they *do* perceive reveals who they are. Two people looking at the same room see different things; the reader should be able to name the viewpoint character from the objects noticed, the words used, and the order they appear.

反例（neutral camera）："房间很乱，一股旧纸的味道。她觉得不安。"
正例（strong filter）："到处堆着纸，她已经在算哪一摞最近被人翻动过。"

Same facts, but the second tells you how this person processes a room.

## Emotion through perception, not label

At high emotion, the character doesn't know they're "afraid" — they know their hands are cold, the other person's voice has gone flat, none of the lines they rehearsed are available now. Render the physical and perceptual experience; let the reader name the feeling.

反例："她感到极度恐惧。"
正例："她想起自己反复练过的那句话，可现在一个字都够不着。"

## Anti-patterns

- "她感到 + 情绪词" in a high-stakes scene.
- The viewpoint character perceiving another's inner state as *fact* rather than inference.
- Narration that sounds like a neutral observer instead of a person in a particular mood.
- Description that is visually accurate but emotionally inert.
