# Subtext Craft — by example

Running two conversations at once: the stated surface exchange and the real meaning underneath.

## Core

Characters rarely say what they mean. They say what they think they're allowed to say, what they believe will get them what they want, or what they can risk right now. The real meaning lives in what is avoided, deflected, or overstated.

## Techniques (each shown, not listed as a rule)

**What the character notices is who they are.** Perception is selective; use the viewpoint character's attention to carry information and undercurrent at once.
> 阿坤进门先看的不是老周，是桌上那只没动过的碗。

**The incidental detail as information carrier.** A fact revealed through a minor element — a photo on a phone screen, food untouched, a book left face-down — lands harder because the reader assembles it.

**Action as statement.** A character straightening papers while being accused is saying something; one who doesn't look up is saying something. The action *is* the subtext — do not explain it.

**The double exchange.** "你吃了吗？" can mean "你还愿意留下吗？". Write the literal exchange without losing the real one underneath.

## Anti-patterns

- A character explaining their own emotional state in interior monologue ("她很生气，但她也知道自己害怕").
- Saying exactly what they feel at the moment of maximum stakes.
- Subtext followed by narration that explains the subtext to the reader.
- Action beats that only mark physical position and don't shift the dynamic.

## Revision test

For each significant line, ask: what is the character actually communicating, and what are they carefully *not* saying? If there's no gap between said and meant, the line is only working at the surface.
