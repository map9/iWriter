# SS10 prose-craft-by-example

## How this skill works

Prose quality is a matter of *shape*, and shape does not survive being turned into a rule. So this skill does not give you a checklist to satisfy — it points you at **examples** of prose doing the thing well, and asks you to write *like that*. Read the reference for the dimension your current passage needs, absorb the contrast between the weak and strong versions, and write toward the strong one.

**Do not turn craft into a countable rule.** "每个句子必须同时承担至少两个功能" is exactly the kind of instruction that makes prose worse, not better — it produces cramped, overworked sentences chasing a quota. The references show what dense, alive prose *looks like*; they never tell you to hit a per-sentence count. If you catch yourself applying a numeric rule to every sentence or paragraph, stop and go back to the examples.

## The dimensions (read on demand in `references/`)

Pick the one your passage actually needs — a fast transition needs none of them; a betrayal scene might need three. Don't apply all of them to every paragraph.

- `references/show-vs-tell.md` — when to let the reader *experience* a feeling vs. when to compress and move on.
- `references/dialogue-craft.md` — exchanges that reveal desire, conceal fear, and shift power.
- `references/character-voice.md` — making each character sound distinguishable without dialogue tags.
- `references/deep-pov.md` — filtering all perception through the viewpoint character's knowledge, mood, and language.
- `references/sensory-grounding.md` — making an important scene physically present, with detail that carries mood or pressure.
- `references/subtext-craft.md` — running the stated exchange and the real one at the same time.
- `references/layered-prose.md` — thin vs. dense prose: how a sentence carries situation, character, and undercurrent together (by example, not by quota).

## How to verify it worked

Compare a drafted passage against the relevant reference's strong example: does it land the feeling through evidence rather than labels, sound like *this* character, and stay physically present where it matters? If a passage reads as generic — it could belong to any story — return to the example and rewrite toward it. Behavior-level pairwise quality checks are the fuller test (see the project's paired-verification plan); here, the example *is* the standard.
