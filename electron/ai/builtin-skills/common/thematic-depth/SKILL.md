# Thematic Depth

Use this skill when brainstorming overall structure, designing major turning points, or generating creative ideas for a story.

## Core Principle

Every story is attempting to answer a question about human experience. Identify that question before designing plot. Scenes, conflicts, and character choices are the story's argument—each should advance or complicate the answer.

A scene can be coherent at the plot level but empty underneath. The goal is scenes that operate simultaneously at three levels:
- Surface: what happens (events, actions)
- Middle: what the characters want and why they can't have it (motivation and conflict)
- Deep: what the story proposes about human experience (theme)

## Finding the real question

Surface questions: Will the character survive? Will they get what they want?

Thematic questions: What does wanting this cost them? What do they discover about themselves in the act of pursuing it? What does the story know about human experience that most stories avoid?

The thematic question should be one that the story cannot answer cleanly. Clean answers produce messages. Irresolvable tensions produce meaning.

## Theme embedded, not delivered

Theme appears in the conflict design, in the choices characters are forced to make, in what the story rewards and what it punishes—not in a speech where a character summarizes what the story means.

## Anti-patterns

- A resolution that answers the thematic question too completely. Life doesn't.
- Theme as moral lesson delivered by a wise character near the end.
- Disconnected themes across character arcs rather than one coherent underlying question.
- Situations designed to be dramatic without asking what they reveal about the human condition.
- A story that knows what its characters do but not what they mean.

## Checklist

- What question is this story trying to answer, and can it be answered cleanly? (If yes, it may be too simple.)
- How does this scene or conflict advance or complicate the thematic question?
- Is the theme embedded in the structure of the conflict, or does it only appear at the end?
- What does this story know about human experience that most stories don't say?
