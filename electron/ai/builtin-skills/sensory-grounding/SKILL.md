# Sensory Grounding

Use this skill to make important scenes feel physically present instead of abstractly emotional.

## Minimum Standard

For each important scene, include at least:

- One visual detail the viewpoint character would actually notice.
- One non-visual sensory detail: sound, touch, smell, taste, temperature, pressure, texture, or bodily sensation.
- A reason the detail matters in the moment. Sensory detail should reveal mood, pressure, danger, intimacy, status, or memory. It is not decoration.

## Character Filter

The same room should feel different through different characters.

Before drafting, ask:

- What is the character trying to avoid noticing?
- What detail does their current emotion make louder?
- What detail would a calmer person ignore, but this character cannot?

## Avoid

- Pure emotion labels: "he felt sad", "she was anxious".
- Abstract atmosphere without physical anchors: "tension filled the room".
- Inventory description that pauses the scene without pressure.
- Applying a sensory checklist to every paragraph.

## When Not To Use

Skip for fast transitions, multi-day summaries, direct exposition, or scenes where speed matters more than embodiment.
